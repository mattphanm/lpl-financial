# Beneficiary Documentation Guide

**Document type:** Internal requirements guide
**Audience:** Financial advisors

> Synthetic internal guidance for the TransferReady prototype. Not investment, tax, or legal advice.

## When a beneficiary form is required

A beneficiary designation form is required for all retirement account transfers (Traditional IRA and Roth IRA). The form names the primary and contingent beneficiaries for the account.

## What blocks processing

**Beneficiary designation forms must be completed before the transfer can proceed to custodian review.** If the beneficiary form has a status of MISSING, the transfer is blocked at the Documentation stage and cannot advance.

A missing beneficiary form is one of the most common reasons an otherwise-complete transfer stalls.

## What the advisor may explain

- That the form is required to complete the transfer.
- Which fields are mandatory (primary beneficiary, allocation percentages).
- That primary allocations must total 100%.
- How the client can return the completed form.

## What the advisor should not provide advice on

- Whether a specific person should be named as a beneficiary.
- Tax consequences of a particular beneficiary designation.
- Legal implications of beneficiary choices, including spousal rights.

If a client asks whether a spouse must be listed, the advisor should explain that this is a personal and potentially legal/tax decision and direct the client to the form instructions or to appropriate professional advice. The advisor should not make the determination for the client.
