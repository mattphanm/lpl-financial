# Transfer Escalation Guide

**Document type:** Internal escalation guide
**Audience:** Financial advisors and transition specialists

> Synthetic internal guidance for the TransferReady prototype. Not investment, tax, or legal advice.

## Inactivity thresholds

Inactivity is measured as the number of days since the transfer's last activity.

- **5+ days inactive:** The transfer is at elevated risk. Send a follow-up to the responsible party (client or internal team).
- **10+ days inactive:** The transfer is high risk. Escalate and use an alternate contact method if the first channel has not produced a response.

## Client vs. internal escalation

The correct escalation path depends on **who the transfer is waiting on**:

- **Waiting on client** (status WAITING_ON_CLIENT, or a MISSING client-action requirement such as a beneficiary form or signature): the next action is **client outreach**. Send a reminder explaining exactly what is needed.
- **Waiting internally** (status INTERNAL_REVIEW, or a REQUESTED internal review / custodian approval requirement): the next action is **internal follow-up**, not client outreach. Contacting the client here adds no value and can cause confusion. Follow up with the internal review queue or the custodian liaison instead.

## When a transition specialist should review

Escalate to a transition specialist when:

- A transfer has been inactive for 10 or more days despite follow-up.
- A requirement cannot be satisfied through normal client outreach (for example, a signature guarantee the client cannot obtain, or an unresolved identity mismatch).
- Multiple required documents are missing and the transfer is not progressing.
- The delivering institution has rejected or delayed the transfer for reasons the advisor cannot resolve directly.
